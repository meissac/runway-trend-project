# Runway Trend Insights

Four insights, each traceable to a specific validated finding from Phase 5 (Trend EDA) and Phase 6 (Categorization Framework). Every insight is written twice: a technical version anchored to methodology, and a business version for the client-facing report.

---

## 1. Eclectic prints are the clearest rising trend in the dataset

**Technical:** "Colorful prints, resort/eclectic" shows the strongest momentum of any style cluster (+13.6pp share gain, early vs. late period), and this finding is robust to exclusion of the 2021 pandemic-disrupted season, which had been inflating — but not creating — the trend. No reliable color-family signature could be established for this cluster after the orange/white bucket was rejected for stage-lighting contamination in Q3.

**Business:** Eclectic, print-heavy collections have been the fastest-growing runway category over the past two decades — a trend that holds even after adjusting for 2021's unusual, pandemic-affected season. We can confidently say prints are rising, but can't yet say *which* colors are driving it.

---

## 2. Tailored menswear-adjacent and monochrome looks are in sustained decline

**Technical:** "Menswear — dark tailoring" (-11.5pp) and "Dark monochrome, mixed silhouette" (-12.0pp) are the two largest decliners. Both carry the strongest independently-verified black color signatures in the dataset (lift 1.32 and 1.53 respectively), meaning this isn't just a labeling artifact — genuinely black-dominant, traditionally tailored looks are losing runway share.

**Business:** Classic, all-black power tailoring — long a runway staple — has been steadily losing ground for years. This isn't a blip; it's the most consistent decline we found in the data, in the two categories with the clearest, most verified color identity of any we studied.

---

## 3. Black is fashion's universal neutral, even inside "colorful" collections

**Technical:** Rank-1 dominant color is black in 43.2% of all looks dataset-wide, and even within "Colorful prints, resort/eclectic" specifically, black accounts for 42% of dominant colors (spot-check confirmed genuine, not a segmentation artifact — 5/6 sampled images showed real black garment presence).

**Business:** Even in the most colorful, eclectic collections, black remains the most common single color choice — functioning as a neutral base that print- and color-driven trends are built on top of, not replacing.

---

## 4. Most "designer reinvention" is actually the industry moving together, not individual houses breaking away

**Technical:** Of 21 designers showing a shift in dominant style cluster (2005+ window, controlled for the early-2000s photography-era confound), only 5 (Christian Dior, Moschino, Marni, Undercover, Versace) moved against the dataset-wide directional trend established in Insights 1/2. The remaining 16 shifted in the same direction the whole industry was already moving.

**Business:** When a designer's style appears to "evolve," it's usually them following where the whole runway is heading, not carving an independent path. Only a handful of houses — Dior, Moschino, Marni, Undercover, and Versace — show a genuine contrarian shift worth flagging as distinctive repositioning.
